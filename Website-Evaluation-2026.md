# Onyx Website Evaluation & Recommendations

**Site reviewed:** https://www.onyxcfo.com/
**Date:** June 2026
**Prepared for:** Josephine Mack, Chief of Staff
**Goal as stated:** Not a lead-generation engine — a *credible, professional place for people who look us up* (prospects doing due diligence, referral recipients, candidates, vendors, banks).

---

## 1. Bottom line

The site is clean and loads fine, but it reads as **out of date and inconsistent with who Onyx is today.** For a "look us up" site, the single most important quality is that everything a visitor finds is *accurate, current, and consistent* with what they hear from you directly. Right now there are several gaps that would make a careful visitor hesitate:

- The site brands as **"Onyx Accounting Group"**, while your current identity (per your master prompts) is **"Onyx | Bookkeeping & CFO Services."**
- The About story ("founded when a Director at a national top-10 firm realized the industry was stuck in the mud") **does not reflect your current leadership and team.**
- There are **no named team members, no photos, no LinkedIn links** — the #1 trust signal for a firm this size is missing.
- The tone in places (the "pilot flying the plane and serving cocktails" analogy) is **more jokey than your stated brand voice** of warm, professional, clear, and confident.
- Content is **thin** — a handful of short pages with no proof, no specifics, and no reason to believe.

None of this is hard to fix. The work is mostly *content and accuracy*, which is exactly what you asked for. Below is the detail, a peer comparison, and a prioritized action list.

---

## 2. What's working (keep these)

- **Clean, uncluttered layout.** Nothing garish; it doesn't look spammy.
- **Clear service categories** exist (CFO & Controller, Startups & Small Business, Cleanup).
- **Contact info is present and correct** (address, hours, phone, email).
- **A real differentiator is hinted at** — the "balance of day-to-day bookkeeping *and* long-term strategic focus, a team rather than one person" idea is genuinely good positioning. It's just under-developed.

---

## 3. Content issues (the main event)

### 3.1 Brand & identity are inconsistent — fix first
- Site name: **"Onyx Accounting Group"** vs. your current **"Onyx | Bookkeeping & CFO Services."** Pick one canonical name and make the entire site, LinkedIn, and email signatures match. Inconsistent naming is the first thing that makes someone doing due diligence wonder if they're looking at the right company or an abandoned site.
- The **About origin story** references an unnamed "Director at a national top-10 firm." Your master prompt describes a specific, credible leadership team (Steven Nikolov – Principal; Lisa Danforth – CFO; Cindi Campbell – Controller; plus the accounting team and Chief of Staff). The site should tell *that* story with *those* people.

### 3.2 No team = the biggest single gap
For a firm of ~1–10 people whose product is *trust in your numbers*, the team is the website. Right now a visitor can't see who would actually handle their books or who's accountable. Add:
- A short, warm bio + headshot for each leader (Principal, CFO, Controller at minimum).
- Credentials and relevant background (years of experience, types of clients, any CPA/industry designations — only state what's accurate).
- LinkedIn links for each person. This alone materially raises credibility for a "look us up" visitor, because that's exactly what they'll do next.

### 3.3 Voice & tone don't match your stated brand
Your brand voice is **warm, professional, organized, no jargon, deliver confidence/clarity/trust.** The "pilot flying the plane and serving cocktails" line is memorable but reads as casual/cute and slightly knocks competitors. Keep *one* plain-language analogy if you like it, but lead with the confident, reassuring tone you've defined. Rewrite page copy to that standard.

### 3.4 Thin content with no proof
Pages are short and assertion-only ("affordable," "unique approach," "we know what bankers want") with nothing backing them up. Add concrete substance:
- **What you actually deliver** each month (e.g., reconciled books by the Nth, monthly financial package, AP/AR managed, cash-flow visibility) — specifics signal competence.
- **Social proof:** 2–4 client testimonials (even first-name + industry), and/or a short anonymized result ("cleaned up 14 months of back books for a multi-entity real estate holding company").
- **Industries/clients you serve well** (real estate holding cos, multi-entity, service businesses, startups) — your ICP from the master prompt is a ready-made section.

### 3.5 Service clarity & boundaries
- State plainly **what's included** in Bookkeeping vs. Controller vs. Fractional CFO, ideally as three tiers so a visitor understands the ladder.
- Add a short, professional **"What we don't do"** note where relevant (you don't file taxes, don't provide legal advice, don't make financial decisions for clients). This actually *builds* trust and prevents mismatched inquiries — and matches your internal service boundaries.
- The current site references "tax" and "accounting" experience loosely; make sure nothing implies you file taxes if you don't.

### 3.6 Missing standard pages / elements
- **FAQ** (How do you price? What software? How do we share files? How fast is onboarding? Do you replace our accountant or work with them?). This answers the due-diligence visitor directly.
- **A simple "How we work" / process** section (your QBO → Karbon → reporting workflow, described in plain client terms).
- **Privacy & data-security note.** You handle financial data; a one-paragraph statement on confidentiality, 1Password/secure file handling, etc. is reassuring and increasingly expected.
- **Footer essentials:** copyright year, address, LinkedIn icon, email/phone repeated.

## 4. How you compare to firms of similar size

Well-regarded small bookkeeping / fractional-CFO firms (the tier you're benchmarked against — think the smaller, credible boutique firms rather than the venture-scale Kruze/Zeni players) consistently do five things on their sites. Here's where Onyx stands:

| Element peers do well | Onyx today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Named team with photos + credentials** | Missing | **High** — this is the biggest differentiator for small firms |
| **Specific deliverables / what you get monthly** | Vague | High |
| **Social proof (testimonials, logos, results)** | None | High |
| **Clear service tiers & pricing approach** | Categories exist, no detail | Medium |
| **Consistent brand & current info** | Inconsistent / stale | **High** |
| **Trust/security & process transparency** | Missing | Medium |
| Clean, professional design | Yes | Low — you're fine here |

The pattern in the better small-firm sites is that they **win on specificity and people, not on flashy design.** They make a visitor feel "these are real, competent humans who will own my books and tell me the truth." That's achievable for Onyx purely through content updates — and it plays directly to your stated strengths (precision, organization, exceptional service, a *team* rather than one person).

## 5. Design ideas (secondary, since you asked for "open to")

You don't need a redesign. A few light touches would modernize it:
- **Add real photography of the team** (even good professional headshots on a neutral background). People photos outperform stock imagery for trust.
- **One strong, calm hero statement** on the homepage that states what you do and who for, in one sentence — e.g., *"Clear, accurate books and CFO-level insight for growing businesses — handled by a dedicated team, not a solo bookkeeper."*
- **Consistent typographic hierarchy and generous white space** — let the professionalism read as calm and organized (mirrors the brand).
- **A subtle, consistent color/brand system** built around the "Onyx" name (deep charcoal/black + a single accent). Make sure the logo and favicon are crisp.
- **Mobile check:** confirm the nav, phone number (click-to-call), and contact form all work well on a phone, since a lot of "look us up" traffic is mobile.
